A smoke detector only sounds once the smoke reaches it. Where you put it matters as much as how many you have. This is what a technician looks at when visiting an apartment.

## How many you need

Start with one detector in each bedroom and one in the hallway that leads to the bedrooms. With doors closed at night, the detector inside the room is the one that wakes whoever sleeps there.

It is worth adding one in the living room and in a home office too, especially where electrical equipment stays on for long hours.

In a duplex or a house, put at least one detector on each floor, near the stairs. Smoke rises, and the staircase is how it reaches the next floor.

## Where to mount them

On the ceiling, close to the middle of the room. Hot smoke rises and spreads along the ceiling, so that is where the detector meets it first.

In the hallway, halfway along, between the bedrooms and the rest of the apartment. From there it covers both the living area and the bedroom doors.

If the ceiling is not an option, some detectors are made for walls. In that case, follow the manufacturer's instructions on height.

## Where not to put them

- **Inside the kitchen.** Steam and cooking smoke set off false alarms. Mount the detector just outside, in the living room or the hallway next to it.
- **In the bathroom.** Shower steam confuses the sensor.
- **Next to the air conditioner or a vent.** The airflow pushes smoke away from the detector.
- **In the corner where wall meets ceiling.** The air there barely moves, so smoke arrives late.

## Testing and upkeep

Every detector has a test button. Press it from time to time and check that you hear a loud beep. The manufacturer's instructions say how often.

A short beep that keeps repeating usually means the battery is running low. Replace it straight away, and do not disconnect the detector while you wait for a new one.

Dust builds up in the openings over time. Clean them gently, without water or cleaning products. If the detector shows a replacement date, replace the whole unit when that date comes.

## When to call a technician

A single detector is easy to fit yourself. A technician is worth calling when:

- there are many rooms or several floors, and you want every detector to sound together when one of them picks up smoke;
- the ceiling is high, or it is not clear where smoke will reach the detector;
- false alarms keep coming back, or the home has old detectors nobody remembers installing;
- the job is a stairwell, car park or storage room in a building.

The technician checks the apartment, suggests how many detectors and where, and installs them. Read more on our [smoke detectors](page:smoke-detectors) page.
